Success is a powerful word that means something different to each person. It's what people dream to achieve in life and some have achieved their idea of success. I define success as the ability to have confidence and meaning from within yourself. These three stories Richard Cory, The lie and on the bridge show that success is less about money, living up to expectations, or being popular and instead is about knowing your own strength and being yourself. 

	Richard Cory is a poem about a man who is an accomplished person and others look up to him. “And he was rich-yes, richer than a king-” (Robinson, line 9). Cory was an accomplished man but at the end of then he ended his life as he wasn't happy about his life. This poem really shows us that success is not only about looks and money seeing that even though Cory was accomplished he wasn't really happy. Similarly,

	The lie is a story about a kid whose parents wanted him to get into a college they owned. He didn't get in and felt bad about lying. “I wanted to tell his father the truth but I couldn`t ”(Vonnegut pg8).  Eli ended up lying to his parents because he was too scared to tell them he failed their expectations. At first he felt bad but after he talked to his mom he realized success isn't just about living up to their expectations but doing what he actually likes. This connects to real life because a lot of teens today feel pressure to look popular, act tough or live up to their parents expectations.

Success is also shown in On the Bridge. This story is about two boys, Adam and Seth who try to act tough to impress others. Seth tries to show confidence by pretending to be someone he isn't. “He tried to act tough, but inside he knew it wasn't him”(Cofer,pg.3) . This shows that acting like somebody you're not does not make you successful. At the end, Seth realizes that being yourself is more important than trying to fit in with the crowd. Success is about having the courage to be true to yourself, even when it is hard.
	When looking at all three texts together, I see a clear message: success is not about what other people think of you. In Richard Cory,  people thought Cory had a perfect life, but he was not truly successful because he lacked inner happiness. In the lie, Eli learned that success could not be defined by his parents expectations, but by what he wanted for himself. In On the Bridge Seth realized that copying others would never bring him real success, only being himself could. These lessons connect to my definition of success as having confidence and meaning from within yourself. 
In conclusion, success is different for everyone, but these three texts prove that money, popularity, or meeting other people's expectations do not guarantee it. These stories remind me that true success doesn't come from impressing others, but from being confident in who you are. That is a lesson that still matters today. Success is about being honest with yourself, knowing your own strength, and finding purpose in life. That kind of success is lasting, because it comes from within yourself and not from what the world expects. In the end these texts show that real success is not about money or popularity, but about having the confidence and inner strength to define  your own life.
